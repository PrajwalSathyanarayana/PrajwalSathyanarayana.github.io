Bank Agent Automation

An LLM learns a task on a legacy banking portal once. After that the task replays with no model in the loop.

2–4 sReplay, no model
$0.19–0.33To learn a task
1,222Tests, none call the model
Problem
Credit unions run back-office portals with no API, and staff move money through them by hand. Letting a model click every time is too slow, too costly and not predictable enough for payments.
Built
A Discovery mode where Claude drives a real browser and every action is saved as a typed, Ed25519-signed artifact, and a Replay mode that runs that artifact deterministically. Payments are checked against the request before the click, and a live handoff gives a person the browser when the run can't safely continue.
Result
Five credit-union tasks, including bill pay, learned once and replayed in 2–4 seconds at zero model cost. Three payments in a row each moved exactly the amount, and a tampered artifact is refused before the browser opens.

02 / 04 · Production ML

Manhattan Taxi Fare Estimation

A cloud-native estimator that gives NYC yellow and green taxi riders an upfront fare before the ride, from pickup and dropoff zone, time and passenger count.

92.2%R² on dollar fare
$5.71Avg. error per trip (RMSE)
~$46Infra cost per month (Load balancer $12 + 2 droplets $14 + managed Postgres $15 + Spaces $5; VPC and Mapbox free tier $0 = $46/month)
Infrastructure Estimator UI
Problem
Ride-sharing apps show a fare before booking; traditional NYC taxis don't, so riders can't compare yellow and green taxi costs upfront. This closes that gap for Manhattan trips.
Data
NYC TLC yellow and green trip records for the trailing 36 months. The full set was 3.2 GB and exceeded available compute, so the model trains on a 5% sample. Zone IDs stand in for addresses, so there is no passenger PII.
Model
LightGBM regression on log-transformed total fare, chosen for non-linear tabular relationships and fast inference. Features: trip distance, passenger count, pickup and dropoff zone, hour, day of week, month and week of year. The 80/20 split is chronological, not random, to respect the time-series nature of the data.
Built
A dockerized FastAPI service (python:3.11-slim, uvicorn) on two DigitalOcean droplets in a private VPC behind a load balancer, with managed PostgreSQL for features, metrics and a prediction cache. Mapbox Directions supplies real driving distance. Secrets live in environment variables, and inputs are validated for zone, passenger count and date/time.
Result
R² 92.2% and RMSE $5.71 on dollar fare; R² 89.2% and RMSE 0.169 on log fare. Dropoff zone, pickup zone and trip distance are the top features by importance.

Predicting the Future: Where Will Stocks, Cryptos, and Commodities Stand by 2026?

Forecasting stock, cryptocurrency and commodity prices through 2026 with linear regression on four years of daily prices, with Apple as the worked example.

0.765R² on Apple
1,011Trading days
$240.74Apple forecast, Jan 2026
Apple · 2026 forecast Bitcoin · same model
Question
Where will stocks, cryptocurrencies and commodities stand by 2026 if their 2020–2024 trend continues, and how much of each price does time alone explain?
Data
Kaggle's US Stock Market and Commodities Data (2020–2024): daily price and volume from Jan 2, 2020 to Jan 31, 2024. Stocks (Apple, Nvidia, Google, Meta, the S&P 500), crypto (Bitcoin, Ethereum) and commodities (natural gas, crude oil, gold, silver, platinum, copper). Mixed and malformed date formats were standardized, a date-ordinal column turned dates into a numeric regression input, and the last two days were trimmed for a clean range.
Model
Simple linear regression of price on time for each asset, plus an OLS regression for inference (coefficients, p-values, confidence intervals). Five custom analysis functions: summary statistics, monthly average price, monthly average plot, monthly percent-change plot and price-distribution histogram.
Result
For Apple, R² = 0.765 (time alone explains 76.5% of price variance), MSE = 266.98. Price rises ~$0.068/day (p < 0.05, 95% CI $0.066–$0.071) from an intercept of $90.84 on Jan 2, 2020 (95% CI $88.85–$92.87). Validation: $125.17 predicted vs. $124.85 actual for May 18, 2021, and $200.25 vs. $191.04 for May 20, 2024. Extrapolated forecast: $240.74 on Jan 2, 2026.

04 / 04 · Data Science

Analyzing English Premier League Match Data

An exploratory visualization project, built as an interactive Shiny dashboard, testing whether weather and betting odds predict EPL match outcomes.

~85%Winners picked by betting odds
3Sources merged
2021–23EPL seasons
Shiny app · odds vs. outcomes Draws vs. temperature
Question
Do weather and betting odds predict how English Premier League matches turn out, and do referees treat home and away teams differently?
Data
Match results, weather and betting odds for the 2021–2023 seasons, each with different time frames and formats. The core engineering work was normalizing dates across formats, joining weather to matches on date and location, and joining odds on date, home team and away team.
Built
An interactive Shiny app: select one team to see its season trend (odds vs. date, opponent on hover, extreme-weather dates highlighted), or two teams for a head-to-head comparison. Visuals include a lollipop chart of odds vs. outcomes, a referee-decision pie chart and a stacked bar chart of results across temperature ranges.
Result
Betting odds picked the winner in about 85% of matches. Upsets were more likely in extreme weather, especially rain, and draws rose at both temperature extremes, plausibly because extreme weather slows play. There was no conclusive referee bias between home and away teams, though some referees were harsher on away teams overall.
